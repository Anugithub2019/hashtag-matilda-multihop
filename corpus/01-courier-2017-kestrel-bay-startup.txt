Kestrel Bay researchers bet on seaweed to fight superbugs
The Calder Courier, 18 April 2017

Two former Kestrel Bay University researchers have set up a company in a converted boatshed on Marina Road, and they think the answer to drug-resistant infections may be growing on the rocks a few hundred metres away.

Tidewell Bio was registered this month by marine biochemist Dr Imogen Varga and engineer Rafael Duarte. The pair spent the past three years studying a deep-red alga that clings to the reef shelf off the northern headland of Kestrel Bay. Extracts from the alga, they say, kill several strains of bacteria that no longer respond to common antibiotics.

"Everyone walks past this weed at low tide," Dr Varga said. "We think it has been fighting bacteria for millions of years, and we want to learn how."

Mr Duarte, who will run the laboratory and the extraction equipment, said the company would spend its first two years confirming which molecule in the alga is responsible. Both founders are paying themselves nothing for now, and the company is funded by a small state innovation grant and savings.

The boatshed still smells of diesel, Dr Varga admitted, but it has running seawater, which a city laboratory would not.
