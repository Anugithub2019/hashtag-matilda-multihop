The dredger's daughter
Coast Magazine, profile, May 2023

Annika Lindqvist was nine the first time her father let her steer a tug across Port Calder harbour. Erik Lindqvist had started Greywater Marine in 1979 with one secondhand dredge and a loan from his brother-in-law. Forty-four years later the company dredges shipping channels along the whole coast and moves the heaviest cargo that arrives at the port.

Lindqvist became chief executive in 2018, when her father retired. She is blunt about how she got there. "Nepotism got me the interview," she says. "Twenty years on the boats got me the job."

She lives in Port Calder with her husband, Samuel Osei, an infectious-diseases physician at St Brigid's Hospital. They met, she says, when he treated a deckhand for an infected hand in 2009.

Her younger brother, Tomas, worked for the company for a decade before leaving to open a restaurant in Kestrel Bay. "He was the better sailor," she says. "He's an even better cook."

Greywater is bidding this year for the inner-harbour dredging contract, the biggest job the port has offered since the 1990s.
